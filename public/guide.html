User Guide

Event Estimate Engine

One place to build event estimates on your real rate cards, route them for approval, track actuals against plan, and hand accounting a file Intacct will accept. This guide follows an estimate from first click to invoice.

01Signing in

The app runs in your browser — no install. Your admin creates your account and gives you your email and password directly (the app doesn't send invite emails during the beta).

What you see depends on your role. Estimators live in Estimates and the Pipeline Dashboard; accounting works the review queue and exports; admins also get Rate Cards, User Management, and System Settings. The full breakdown is in Who can do what.

The left sidebar is the map: Pipeline Dashboard for the big picture, Estimates for the list, Rate Cards for pricing, AI Scoping to draft an estimate from a description.

Corporate-produced events skip the accounting review lane and go straight from recap to invoiced. An estimate can also be marked lost or cancelled from most stages — both ask for a reason.

One more thing worth knowing: status lives on each location segment, not the estimate as a whole. A multi-city event can have Dallas in recap while Chicago is still active, and each moves on its own.

03Create an estimate

  1. Go to Estimates and click New Estimate.
  2. Pick the client — this decides which rate card prices everything.
  3. Name the event, set dates, city and state, and choose the cost structure: Corporate (DriveShop produces it) or Office (a regional office produces it and receives a payout share). This changes how cost and margin are calculated, so pick it up front.
  4. Click Begin Estimating to move it from pipeline into estimating.

Prefer to start from history? AI Scoping can draft the whole thing — see the AI tools. And any existing estimate can be duplicated from the list to reuse its structure.

04Build it out

An estimate is four kinds of content, each with its own tab.

Schedule

A day-by-day staffing calendar. Add dates, add staff rows from the rate card, and click cells to fill hours. Labor totals can drive off this grid directly.

Labor Log

The roles view: each row is a role with days, quantity, and day rate from the rate card. Use it when you plan by role rather than by calendar.

Line Items

Everything that isn't labor, in sections: Travel, Creative, Production, Logistics, Miscellaneous, and Fees & Markups. Pass-through sections bill at cost plus the client's markup.

Summary

The P&L: revenue, cost, and gross profit by section, with the agency fee applied. What the client PDF is built from.

Above the tabs, five cards track the money live as you work: Gross Revenue, Net Revenue, Total Cost, GP, and GP%. If GP% drops below the company threshold, it turns amber — that's your early warning before approval sees it.

A few behaviors to expect

  • The agency fee line is created automatically for clients that carry one. Don't add it by hand.
  • Multi-city events: use Add Location. Each location gets its own schedule, labor, and workflow status.
  • Items picked from the rate card carry their price and accounting codes with them. Custom items can be typed in, but they start at $0 — and $0 lines block approval, which is the next section.

05Where prices come from

Every client has a rate card — the agreed price list for that client, loaded from your master pricing. When you add an item, its rate comes from there. Rate cards are managed by admins under Rate Cards; estimators don't edit prices, they use them.

Three pricing rules the app enforces

  • "No rate on file" — an item the client has no agreed price for is marked in amber in the picker. You can still add it, but the estimate cannot be submitted for review while any line has a zero or missing rate. Price it or remove it.
  • "Billed at markup" — travel, and most production and logistics items, are pass-throughs: they bill at actual cost plus the client's markup percentage, so they deliberately have no fixed rate. This is not an error.
  • Overtime lives on the parent item (e.g. Event Director Days carries its own OT rate). You don't add overtime as a separate line.

Office vs. corporate cost

For office events, cost is the office's payout share of the rate (typically 75%). For corporate events, cost comes from the rate card's corporate cost (typically 50% of rate; 100% on pass-throughs). Flipping the Corporate/Office toggle recalculates every labor row — margins will visibly change, and that's correct.

06The AI tools

Three assists, all optional, all grounded in DriveShop's own historical events (about 1,700 past estimates).

AI Intelligence panel

The collapsible right-hand panel inside an estimate. It reads your numbers and flags things worth a look — staffing that doesn't match the schedule, ratios out of line with similar past events. Nudges are suggestions, never blockers.

Chat

Ask questions about the open estimate in plain English from the same panel. History clears when you leave the page.

AI Scoping

Describe an event — client, type, size — and it drafts a full scope priced on that client's rate card, using similar past events as reference. One click turns the draft into a real estimate you then edit like any other.

Good habit

Treat AI output as a first draft from a well-read colleague. It knows the history; you know this event. Review before you submit.

07Approval

When the estimate is ready, click Submit for Review. The confirmation tells you exactly who it's going to.

  1. Account manager gate — the client's assigned approver (or all AMs if none is set) reviews and approves.
  2. CFO gate — only for estimates over the approval threshold set in System Settings.
  3. Client gate — client sign-off, recorded manually during the beta (see Beta notes).

Approved means active: the plan locks and the event runs. If the reviewer wants changes, Send Back to Estimate returns it with everything editable. Every submit, approval, and version is kept in the history panel — nothing is lost when things bounce back.

08Recap & change orders

After the event: recap

Move the segment to recap and enter what actually happened. Planned numbers stay untouched — actuals are recorded alongside them, so the Summary tab shows estimated vs. actual with variance.

  • Schedule hours pre-fill from plan; correct only what changed.
  • Things that weren't planned get added with the rose + Add Unplanned buttons. They're badged, kept out of planned totals, and land as visible overrun — never buried.
  • Receipts attach to pass-through line items, feeding the Receipt Backup Packet and accounting review.
  • Every staff row needs a real person's name before the segment can be invoiced. The app blocks it otherwise.

Mid-event changes: change orders

If scope changes while the event is active, use Request Edit for small corrections, or Create Change Order for a formal, numbered CO. The app computes the delta from the approved baseline automatically, and the CO routes through approval like the original.

Accounting review — office events

Office-produced events must pass accounting before invoicing: Submit to Accounting → accounting reviews actuals and receipts → Approve Recap moves it to export ready. Corrections can be requested, which sends it back with notes.

Intacct CSVs — office events at export ready

Two files, matching accounting's upload templates exactly: Download AR Invoice Upload CSV (what the client owes) and Download AP Bill Upload CSV (what the office is owed). Customer IDs, payment terms, item codes, and GL accounts fill in automatically from the accounting setup. Every download is logged.

Before an export will pass

The estimate itself needs four things filled in: a revenue segment (this is also the Intacct department), a project ID, and the event city and state. The export check names anything missing rather than producing a bad file.

10Admin

Rate Cards

Per-client pricing, client settings (markup %, agency fee %, office payout %, approver), contacts, and bulk import from spreadsheet.

User Management

Create accounts and assign roles. You set the password and pass it on directly — no invite email in the beta.

System Settings

The GP% warning threshold and the CFO approval threshold. Changes apply to the next submission.

Accounting Setup

The Intacct mappings behind exports: item catalog, office vendor profiles, revenue segments, customer IDs. Rarely touched once set.

12Beta notes

Honest limits of the current release, so nothing surprises you mid-demo.

  • Client approval emails are off. Internal approvals work end to end; the client's sign-off is recorded manually by your team until the DriveShop email domain is verified.
  • Notifications are in-app only — the bell in the header, not your inbox.
  • Start with office events. They're the fully exercised path, including the accounting lane and Intacct export.
  • Volvo MS pricing is seeded from Volvo as a starting point — adjust its rate card if its program rates differ.
  • Professional Chauffeur bills at the standard-market rate for now; the high-market rate needs a second Intacct item code before it can bill separately.
One rule during beta

Don't send a client-facing PDF from an estimate that's in recap with unplanned additions — the labor rollup on that specific combination is a known issue being consolidated. Estimate-stage and approval-stage PDFs are exact.

Found something off? The Submit Feedback page goes straight to the team, and screenshots help.
